02 · The situation

Why this is being asked now

Three things happened in Colombia at once, and only one of them is about MicroAire.

  • The cost of money rose sharply. The policy rate moved from 9.25% to 12.00% inside a single year and held there. At that level a surgeon does not decline the equipment, he defers it, and a deferral is indistinguishable from a lost sale until too late.
  • The peso appreciated, which made the equipment roughly 17% cheaper in local terms and should have helped. It did not, because the constraint was never the price.
  • Service remained unresolved. With no certified station in the country, an out-of-service handpiece is an idle theatre, and the cost of that attaches to the brand rather than to the distributor.

The problem stated in one line

The binding constraint on MicroAire's Colombian revenue is not demand, price, or the exchange rate. It is financing at the point of decision and service after the sale. Neither is a manufacturing problem, and neither can be solved by the distributor alone.

It also makes the commitment checkable rather than aspirational. ACT commits to a floor of USD 526,000. Actual 2025 purchases were USD 522,500. The two are 0.7% apart: the commitment is the number already delivered, and MicroAire can verify it without taking anyone's word for it.

04 · Options considered

Three courses of action, priced

A case that presents only its own preferred option is not a case. These are the three available to MicroAire, with the same model applied to each.

OptionWhat it meansNPV to MicroAireVerdict
A · Continue as is No exclusivity, no support. Revenue holds at the floor and the service and financing constraints remain in place. 0Baseline. Not neutral: the constraint persists.
B · Exclusivity alone Recognise ACT as sole distributor but grant none of the four requests. Structure improves; the binding constraint does not move. -43,306 Modelled at half the growth rate. Negative.
C · Exclusivity with the four requests The proposal as written. Service, terms, training and congress support, against a committed floor. 183,104 Recommended.

Why option B is the interesting one

Granting exclusivity while withholding the support is the middle path, and it is the one that looks safest from a distance. The model says it is the worst of the three: it incurs the transition while leaving untouched the two things actually suppressing revenue. If MicroAire is not prepared to fund the four requests, option A is a more coherent decision than option B.

07 · Break-even

What has to be true for this to pay for itself

This is the number we would check first if we were on your side of the table.

The support pays for itself here

The four requests cost an average of USD 60,384 a year. At a 60% gross margin they are repaid by USD 100,640 of additional annual purchases.

Against a floor of USD 526,000 that is an increase of 19%. The proposal forecasts 15% in year one alone, and the channel delivered 48% between 2024 and 2025 with none of this support in place.

Expressed in equipment rather than dollars, the same threshold is roughly 4 additional console placements a year, against the 22 recorded in 2025.

The comparison that matters

On the credit line alone ACT commits about USD 49,718 a year against MicroAire's USD 60,384. For every dollar MicroAire puts into this arrangement, ACT puts in 0.82 before anything else is counted.

The credit line is the part that can be measured. The rest is already built and already paid for:

  • The commercial infrastructure. Sales teams in five cities, the brand's site in this market, the content library, and an advisory line answered by a person.
  • The congress presence. ACT designs, brands, builds and staffs the stand at the national congress and at the two largest international meetings held in Colombia.
  • The service partner. Identified, compared and prepared ahead of any commitment from MicroAire.
  • The currency risk. Invoiced in USD, collected in COP across the credit period. That gap sits entirely on ACT's balance sheet, at a reference rate of 3,361.62.
  • The floor itself. ACT commits to USD 526,000 whether or not the four requests are granted. The commitment is not conditional on the support.

The assumption that carries the most weight, solved backwards

The 60% gross margin is our estimate and we cannot verify it. So rather than defend it, we solved for the point where it stops working.

This case is positive at any MicroAire gross margin above 35.7%. If your true figure is above that, the conclusion holds whatever else you adjust. If it is below, tell us and we will withdraw the model rather than argue with it.

The case that fails, stated plainly

At half the assumed growth the NPV is negative. That row is not hidden, because it is the honest boundary of this proposal and it says something useful: the four requests are not a subsidy that pays off regardless. They pay off if the channel grows at roughly half the rate it grew in 2025 or better. That is the condition, and it is the one worth arguing about.

14 · Reporting and governance

How MicroAire sees whether this is working

A commitment without visibility is a promise. ACT proposes to report quarterly, on figures MicroAire can reconcile against its own order records.

  • Purchases against the committed floor, cumulative and by quarter.
  • Installed base movement: placements, renewals, and the consumable revenue trailing them.
  • Service performance: repair turnaround times, once the station is live.
  • Pipeline: training sessions delivered, congress activity, and surgeons reached.
  • Territory intelligence: competitor movements and pricing observed in the field.

The point of reporting it this way

Every metric above is one MicroAire can verify independently. If ACT is wrong about this market, the reporting shows it early and cheaply, while the total exposure is still the annual support cost of roughly USD 60,384.

15 · Term and performance

How long, and what happens if ACT misses

The model runs five years, so ACT is asking for a five-year exclusive agreement for the PAL line in Colombia. A shorter term does not work: the service station, the training calendar and the university programme all pay back beyond year two, and no distributor finances a five-year build on a one-year agreement.

The obvious question is what happens if ACT does not deliver. Rather than wait to be asked, ACT proposes the condition itself.

Proposed performance condition

Exclusivity reviewable at the end of any contract year in which ACT's purchases fall below 90% of the committed floor, measured against MicroAire's own order records and reported quarterly.

ACT is comfortable proposing that because it was modelled: at 90% of the floor the arrangement still returns USD 18,185 of net present value to MicroAire. The condition protects MicroAire without putting the agreement on a hair trigger.

ACT asks one reciprocal condition: that any review consider whether the four requests were in place and operating during the year in question. A floor missed because the service station never opened is a different fact from a floor missed because ACT did not sell.

17 · The decision requested

What we are asking MicroAire to approve

Option C: recognise ACT as sole distributor for the PAL line in Colombia, and fund the four requests, against a committed floor of USD 526,000 and quarterly reporting.

The exposure, in one sentence

MicroAire's total downside is the one-off USD 42,000 plus roughly USD 60,384 a year, visible quarterly and reversible at any renewal point. The modelled upside is USD 183,104 of net present value and USD 1,054,994 of incremental purchases across five years.

We are not asking MicroAire to take a risk on an unknown partner in an unfamiliar market. We are asking it to fund four specific things in a channel that grew 48% without them.
